Deployed swarms usually replicate one model (\cref{fig:swarm}). Take ten replicas of Llama-3.2-3B, the weakest roster model, facing a coherent bloc at $f=0.5$. \race\ scores \swarmHomLlamaRace\% (\aip: \swarmHomLlamaAip\%, receiver alone: \swarmHomLlamaSelf\%). With ten replicas of Qwen-3.8-27B the figures are \swarmHomQwenRace\%, \swarmHomQwenAip\% and \swarmHomQwenSelf\%. In a homogeneous swarm the honest agents are one source, and clone tempering counts them once. \race's gain over the receiver therefore comes entirely from what the liars leak: each lie removes one wrong option. Without tempering, the ten identical honest votes swamp that signal (\swarmHomLlamaNoclone\% for Llama, equal to the receiver alone). Adding the two weak-tier models to the roster (``7 + weak arm'') gives \swarmWeakRace\% for \race\ against \swarmWeakAip\% for \aip.

Swarm size helps \race\ because larger swarms contain more \emph{distinct} honest models. From 5 to 40 agents it rises from \sizeCohRaceFive\% to \sizeCohRaceFourZero\% against a coherent bloc, and from \sizeGateRaceFive\% to \sizeGateRaceFourZero\% against gate-aware liars (pooled over $f\in\{.3,.5,.7\}$). \aip\ moves from \sizeCohAipFive\% to \sizeCohAipFourZero\% and from \sizeGateAipFive\% to \sizeGateAipFourZero\%.

One trade-off recurs. On the weak four-model roster, clone tempering costs \race\ accuracy against a perfectly coherent bloc: \swarmWeakRosterRace\% versus \swarmWeakRosterNoclone\% without tempering, with the known-channel oracle at \swarmWeakRosterOracle\%. Counting the bloc's identical vetoes separately happens to help when the bloc is perfectly coordinated. The tempered rule is the one that generalises to real LLM deceivers (\cref{sec:llm}).
