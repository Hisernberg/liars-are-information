\begin{abstract}
When some agents in a multi-agent LLM system lie, the classical defence is to discard them. That defence fails once liars are half the swarm. Prior work showed that a \emph{coherent} liar can be read backwards instead, but it gated this inversion on coherence and then proved that no coherence-based rule survives an adversary coordinating just below the gate. We argue that the informative quantity is \emph{dependence on the truth}. It can be estimated without labels if the estimator is anchored on the one fact a receiver knows: that it is itself honest. We introduce \race\ (Receiver-Anchored Channel Estimation). Each honest agent fits an anchored latent-truth model to its own unlabelled history, tempers replicated channels by error-conditioned clone detection, and gives every peer a signed log-odds weight. This weight subsumes the trust/discard/invert gate.

We prove three things. Against a known-channel receiver a lie can only add information, so there is no breakdown point below $f=1$. Label-free estimators fail at $f\ge1/2$ by label switching. A receiver anchor provably repairs this, and a near-chance anchor provably cannot. We evaluate on \nWorldsReplay\ replayed worlds (six benchmarks, nine open-weight models, fourteen symbolic attacks, four attacks written by LLMs prompted to deceive) and on a live six-model swarm. At $f=0.7$, \race\ scores \zooGateRaceSeven\% where the gate-aware adversary drives the prior mechanism to \zooGateAipSeven\%. Against real LLM deceivers it scores \llmRaceSeven\%, versus \llmAipSeven\% for the prior mechanism, \llmMajSeven\% for majority vote and \llmSelfSeven\% for self-trust, with \llmLosses\ significant losses in \llmCells\ comparisons. Classical crowdsourcing estimators (IWMV, MACE, GLAD, KOS, Dawid--Skene) fall to at most \crowdClassicalMaxSeven\% in the same regime, which isolates the receiver anchor as the ingredient that matters. Against seven independent liars out of ten, \race\ even beats an oracle that knows who lies and removes them (\extRaceIndepSeven\% versus \extRemovalIndepSeven\%). Its label-free channel estimates correlate $r=\chanCorr$ with the truth. In a live swarm of six small models it lifts every honest agent on MMLU, by \liveMmluGainMin--\liveMmluGainMax\ points. On binary BoolQ the frozen method fell below the receiver alone, because small models answer yes/no questions with an option bias that a symmetric channel cannot represent. Class-conditional channels for binary questions, which we adopted after this observation and report as such, recover the loss. We report this and the other failure modes (camouflage, sleepers, weak anchors) and an audit that re-derives every number of the prior artifact from repaired data.
\end{abstract}
