\begin{abstract}
When some agents in a multi-agent LLM system lie, the classical defence is to discard them. That defence fails once liars are half the swarm. Prior work showed that a \emph{coherent} liar can be read backwards instead, but it gated this inversion on coherence and then proved that no coherence-based rule survives an adversary coordinating just below the gate. We argue that the informative quantity is \emph{dependence on the truth}. It can be estimated without labels if the estimator is anchored on the one fact a receiver knows: that it is itself honest. We introduce \race\ (Receiver-Anchored Channel Estimation). Each honest agent fits an anchored latent-truth model to its own unlabelled history, tempers replicated channels by error-conditioned clone detection, and gives every peer a signed log-odds weight. This weight subsumes the trust/discard/invert gate.

We prove three things. Against a known-channel receiver a lie can only add information, so there is no breakdown point below $f=1$. Label-free estimators fail at $f\ge1/2$ by label switching. A receiver anchor provably repairs this, and a near-chance anchor provably cannot. We evaluate on \nWorldsReplay\ replayed worlds (six benchmarks, nine open-weight models, fourteen symbolic attacks, four attacks written by LLMs prompted to deceive) and on a live six-model swarm. At $f=0.7$, \race\ scores \zooGateRaceSeven\% where the gate-aware adversary drives the prior mechanism to \zooGateAipSeven\%. Against real LLM deceivers it scores \llmRaceSeven\%, versus \llmAipSeven\% for the prior mechanism, \llmMajSeven\% for majority vote and \llmSelfSeven\% for self-trust, with \llmLosses\ significant losses in \llmCells\ comparisons. Against seven independent liars out of ten, \race\ even beats an oracle that knows who lies and removes them (\extRaceIndepSeven\% versus \extRemovalIndepSeven\%). Its label-free channel estimates correlate $r=\chanCorr$ with the truth. We report its failure modes (camouflage, sleepers, weak anchors) and an audit that re-derives every number of the prior artifact from repaired data.
\end{abstract}
