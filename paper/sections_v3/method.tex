\section{Receiver-anchored channel estimation}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Model}
For receiver $i$ and question $t$, let $\mathcal S_t$ be the candidate set: the label space for closed questions (C options), or the distinct answers heard on $t$ for open questions. Two open answers are merged when an equivalence checker says they denote the same value. This needs no gold (\cref{sec:protocol}). Let $K_t=|\mathcal S_t|$. \race\ fits the symmetric one-coin latent-truth model
\begin{equation}
Y_t\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal S_t),\qquad
P(R_{tj}=Y_t)=a_j,\qquad P(R_{tj}=c\mid Y_t\neq c)=\frac{1-a_j}{K_t-1},
\end{equation}
with one accuracy $a_j$ per channel, including the receiver's own. Channels are conditionally independent given $Y_t$, up to the clone tempering described below. The posterior score of candidate $c$ is a \emph{signed} weighted vote:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
s_t(c)=\sum_j w_{tj}\,\mathbf 1[R_{tj}=c]\,\lambda_{tj},\qquad
\lambda_{tj}=\log\frac{(K_t-1)\,a_j}{1-a_j}.
\end{equation}
A report with $a_j>1/K_t$ is evidence \emph{for} the reported answer (TRUST). A report with $a_j<1/K_t$ is evidence \emph{against} it, spread over the alternatives (INVERT). $a_j=1/K_t$ contributes nothing (DISCARD). \aip's gate is a three-level quantisation of \cref{eq:score}. Unlike \aip, \race\ decides the sign from $a_j$, not from how coherent a channel is (\cref{prop:quantisation}).

\subsection{Anchored EM}
The parameters are fitted by EM~\citep{dempster1977} on the receiver's own unlabelled history. Label-free latent-class models are identified only up to a relabelling of the latent truth (\cref{prop:switching}). When a coherent bloc holds a plurality, the likelihood prefers the relabelling in which the liars are the experts. \race\ removes the ambiguity with the receiver's self-knowledge, in three ways:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Anchor initialisation.} The first E-step puts posterior mass $0.75$ on the receiver's own answer, not on the plurality.
\item \textbf{Anchor prior.} $a_i\sim\mathrm{Beta}$ with mean $0.75$ and strength $8$. Peers get a weak prior centred at chance (strength $2$), so an unfamiliar peer starts at DISCARD, not TRUST.
\item \textbf{Anchor constraint.} After every M-step $a_i$ is projected to $\ge \bar{1/K}+0.05$, where $\bar{1/K}$ is the chance rate on the tasks the receiver answered.
\end{enumerate}
The M-step is the MAP update $a_j=(\sum_t \omega_t q_t(R_{tj}) + \kappa_j m_j)/(\sum_t \omega_t + \kappa_j)$, where $q_t$ is the current posterior and $\omega_t$ an optional task weight (exponential forgetting in the online variant). The E-step is \cref{eq:score} followed by a softmax. Accuracies are clipped to $[10^{-3},1-10^{-3}]$. At prediction time unseen agents carry zero weight, and ties go to the receiver's own answer.

\subsection{Binary questions: class-conditional channels (v3.1)}\label{sec:binary}
On binary questions \race\ replaces the one-coin channel with a class-conditional one, the two-coin model of \citet{raykar2010}: each channel $j$ has a $2\times2$ confusion matrix $\pi_j[y,r]=P(R_{tj}=r\mid Y_t=y)$, and the truth has a class prior $\rho$. Each channel then has two parameters rather than one, and a short history identifies them. The anchor carries over. The receiver's confusion matrix gets a Dirichlet prior with diagonal mean $0.75$ and strength $8$, peers get a flat Dirichlet prior of strength $2$, and every diagonal entry of the receiver's matrix is kept $\ge 1/2+0.05$ after each M-step. This is the row-diagonal dominance that makes the anchor identifying in \cref{prop:switching}(c). A report $r$ adds $w_{tj}\log(\pi_j[r,r]/\pi_j[\bar r,r])$ to the log-odds of $r$, where $\bar r$ is the other answer. We summarise a channel by half its log diagnostic odds ratio,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary}
\lambda_j=\tfrac12\log\frac{\pi_j[0,0]\,\pi_j[1,1]}{\pi_j[0,1]\,\pi_j[1,0]},
\end{equation}
which is positive for an informative channel, negative for an inverter and close to $0$ for a channel that says ``yes'' regardless of the truth. The one-coin model cannot tell that last channel from a coin flip, and it mis-weights channels that are informative only when they answer against their bias. For more than two options the one-coin model is kept. A $K\times K$ matrix per channel is over-parameterised for the histories we use. Averaged per benchmark, the full-confusion variant is never more than $0.1$ points better than \race\ on four-option questions in any study, and it is far worse in the live swarm.

\emph{Provenance.} This rule is an amendment. \race\ v3.0 used the one-coin model for every answer space and was frozen before the confirmation sets were evaluated. The live swarm (\cref{sec:live}) showed that small models answer binary questions with a strong option bias, and that v3.0 fell below the receiver alone there. v3.1 differs from v3.0 only on binary questions. Every study was re-run with v3.1, and v3.0 is reported alongside it as \texttt{race\_onecoin}. On the replayed BoolQ worlds of E1--E3 the two versions differ significantly in \binaryReplayNonTies\ of \binaryReplayCells\ cells, always in favour of v3.1 (\cref{tab:binary}). The E8 BoolQ result for v3.1 is therefore not a confirmation. The pre-registered E10 run on fresh answers is (\cref{sec:live2}).

\subsection{Clone tempering}
A swarm usually replicates models. A coordinated bloc is, by construction, many copies of one lie. Under conditional independence both are over-counted. \race\ groups channels and tempers each report by $w_{tj}=1/(\text{number of heard members of $j$'s group on $t$})$, so a group contributes one unit of evidence per question. Groups are complete-link clusters over two kinds of link:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Same-source links}: raw agreement $\ge 0.99$ over co-observed history, i.e.\ essentially identical answer streams. These are applied \emph{before} any fit, so a receiver's own replica can never be counted as independent confirmation of the receiver.
\item \textbf{Dependent-error links}: agreement $\ge 0.95$ restricted to history tasks where at least one member is wrong relative to a first-stage truth estimate, with at least 8 such tasks.
\end{itemize}
Raw agreement is the wrong statistic for the second kind: two distinct but accurate models agree on almost every task simply because both are usually right (\cref{prop:clones}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\race\ fit for one receiver $i$ (history $\mathcal H$, no labels)}\label{alg:race}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Encode reports $R_{tj}$ over candidate sets $\mathcal S_t$; $K_t\gets|\mathcal S_t|$
\State $G\gets$ complete-link groups of same-source links (raw agreement $\ge 0.99$)
\State $(a,q)\gets\textsc{AnchoredEM}(R, G)$ \Comment{init at $i$'s answers; Beta prior; $a_i\ge\bar{1/K}+\delta$}
\State $\hat y_t\gets\arg\max_c q_t(c)$
\State $G\gets$ complete-link groups of same-source $\cup$ dependent-error links w.r.t.\ $\hat y$
\State $(a,q)\gets\textsc{AnchoredEM}(R, G)$
\State \Return $a$, $G$ \Comment{predict with \cref{eq:score}, tempering within $G$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Cost.} One EM iteration is $O(|\mathcal H|\cdot N\cdot K)$. With 80 history tasks and 10 agents a fit converges in 10--20 iterations and takes a few milliseconds. The live-swarm run (\cref{sec:live}) spends about three orders of magnitude more time on generating answers than on aggregating them.

\paragraph{Hyper-parameters.} All were fixed before any evaluation data were inspected and were never tuned: anchor mean $0.75$, anchor strength $8$, anchor margin $0.05$, peer strength $2$, thresholds $0.99/0.95$, minimum support $8$, clip $10^{-3}$. Two design decisions were revised after inspecting development data. Both revisions are recorded in \cref{app:devlog}, and the superseded variants appear in every table. The method was then frozen as v3.0, and two confirmation sets that had not been inspected were evaluated (\cref{sec:llm,sec:live}). The binary rule of \cref{sec:binary} (v3.1) was added after the second confirmation set, and it is reported as such.
