\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}

Let $Y$ be the truth on a question, $H$ the honest reports (including the receiver's), and $B$ the Byzantine reports. An adversary strategy $\sigma$ is any conditional law $B\sim\sigma(\cdot\mid Y,H)$, so the adversary may be gold-aware and rushing.

\begin{theorem}[A lie can only add information; the minimax attack is silence]\label{thm:silence}
Let $\mathrm{acc}^*(\sigma)=\E[\max_y P_\sigma(Y{=}y\mid H,B)]$ be the accuracy of the Bayes receiver that knows the joint law induced by $\sigma$, and $\mathrm{acc}^*_H=\E[\max_y P(Y{=}y\mid H)]$. Then $\mathrm{acc}^*(\sigma)\ge\mathrm{acc}^*_H$ for every $\sigma$, with equality whenever $B\perp Y\mid H$. Hence $\inf_\sigma\mathrm{acc}^*(\sigma)=\mathrm{acc}^*_H$, and the infimum is attained, for example by answering at random or by echoing a random honest report.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$P(Y{=}y\mid H)=\E[P(Y{=}y\mid H,B)\mid H]$. The maximum is convex, so by Jensen $\max_y\E[\cdot\mid H]\le\E[\max_y\cdot\mid H]$. Take expectations. If $B\perp Y\mid H$, the two posteriors coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No breakdown point for a known-channel receiver]
Since $\mathrm{acc}^*_H$ is at least the receiver's own accuracy, the known-channel receiver never falls below ``trust yourself'', at any $f<1$. By contrast, any plurality rule reaches $0$ against a coherent bloc once $f>1/2$.
\end{corollary}
Theorem~\ref{thm:silence} is Blackwell's comparison of experiments~\citep{blackwell1953} read adversarially. Its consequence is that robustness is an \emph{estimation} problem. The adversary has exactly three levers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item estimation error;
\item violating the model's assumptions, namely conditional independence given $Y$ and stationarity;
\item withholding its own information.
\end{enumerate}
\Cref{sec:results} is organised around these levers.

\begin{proposition}[Label switching, and what the anchor buys]\label{prop:switching}
(a) \emph{Binary one-coin model.} For $Y$ uniform on $\{0,1\}$ and conditionally independent reports with accuracies $\theta=(a_1,\dots,a_n)$, the report law under $\theta$ equals the law under $\theta'=(1-a_1,\dots,1-a_n)$. If a coherent always-wrong bloc holds a majority, any rule that selects the labelling in which the plurality is accurate outputs the lie on every question. This covers majority vote, plurality-initialised EM and label-free Dawid--Skene.
(b) \emph{Dawid--Skene.} For any permutation $\tau$ of latent classes, relabelling the class prior and the rows of every confusion matrix by $\tau$ leaves the report law unchanged. Uniqueness results~\citep{kruskal1977,allman2009} identify the model only up to such $\tau$.
(c) \emph{Anchor.} If the receiver's confusion matrix is strictly row-diagonally dominant, $\pi_i[k,k]>\pi_i[k,l]$ for all $l\neq k$, then among all relabellings of the true parameters only $\tau=\mathrm{id}$ keeps $\pi_i$ row-diagonally dominant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof of (c)]
For $\tau\neq\mathrm{id}$ pick $k$ with $m=\tau^{-1}(k)\neq k$. Row $k$ of the relabelled matrix is row $m$ of $\pi_i$. Its largest entry is in column $m\neq k$, so dominance fails in row $k$.
\end{proof}
The anchor is also \emph{necessary}. In the binary case with $a_i=1/2$, $\theta$ and $\theta'$ both satisfy any constraint the truth satisfies. A near-chance receiver therefore cannot identify the truth from its own history, whatever estimator it uses. We observe exactly this weak-anchor capture on open-answer questions (\cref{sec:limits}).

\begin{theorem}[Anchored estimation is identified and consistent]\label{thm:consistency}
Consider the class-conditional model: questions i.i.d.\ with a class prior $\rho$ of full support on $K$ classes, and $G\ge3$ channel groups that are conditionally independent given $Y$, each group counted once after clone tempering. Suppose three of the groups have invertible confusion matrices; for a one-coin channel this means accuracy $a\ne1/K$, so a consistent liar counts. Suppose also that the receiver's confusion matrix is row-diagonally dominant with margin $\delta>0$, i.e.\ $\pi_i[k,k]\ge\pi_i[k,l]+\delta$ for all $l\ne k$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the parameters $(\rho,\pi_1,\dots,\pi_G)$ are identified from the law of the reports, exactly and not merely up to a relabelling of the classes, within the set $\Theta_\delta$ where the receiver is $\delta$-dominant;
\item the maximum-likelihood estimator over $\Theta_\delta$, with entries clipped to $[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]$, is strongly consistent as the history length $T\to\infty$, and so is the anchored MAP estimator, whose fixed-strength priors vanish relative to $T$;
\item the plug-in decision converges almost surely to the decision of the known-channel receiver, so the accuracy of the anchored estimator converges to $\mathrm{acc}^*$ of \cref{thm:silence} under the model.
\end{enumerate}
For binary questions this means three channels that are informative in either direction, i.e.\ whose ``yes'' rates differ between yes- and no-questions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) The joint law of the three groups is a three-way array $\sum_y\rho_y\,\pi_1[y,\cdot]\otimes\pi_2[y,\cdot]\otimes\pi_3[y,\cdot]$ whose factor matrices have full Kruskal rank $K$, and $3K\ge2K+2$. By Kruskal's uniqueness theorem~\citep{kruskal1977}, as applied to latent-class models by \citet{allman2009}, it determines $\rho$ and the three confusion matrices up to one simultaneous permutation $\tau$ of the classes; the row-stochastic normalisation fixes the scales. Every further group $g$ is then identified from its joint law with group 1, $\pi_1^{\top}\operatorname{diag}(\rho)\,\pi_g$, because $\pi_1$ and $\operatorname{diag}(\rho)$ are invertible. By \cref{prop:switching}(c) only $\tau=\mathrm{id}$ keeps the receiver dominant. (b) $\Theta_\delta$ with clipping is compact, the log-likelihood is continuous in the parameters, and by (a) the true parameter is its unique maximiser in expectation, so the standard consistency argument for maximum likelihood applies. The Beta and Dirichlet priors have fixed strength and are therefore $O(1/T)$ relative to the likelihood. (c) The posterior is continuous in the parameters, and the Bayes decision changes only on a set of reports of measure zero.
\end{proof}
The theorem is about the estimator, not the algorithm. EM converges to a stationary point, and the anchor initialisation is what places it in the basin of the dominant solution. It has two further consequences. First, identification needs three informative groups. With only two, as in a swarm of the receiver plus one peer, the binary class-conditional model is not identified even with an anchor, and our unit tests reproduce this. Second, if the model is misspecified, the estimator converges to the Kullback--Leibler projection of the truth onto the model. This is what happened to the one-coin model on option-biased live BoolQ answers (\cref{sec:live}), and it is why v3.1 uses class-conditional channels on binary questions (\cref{sec:binary}).

\begin{proposition}[AIP's gate quantises the Bayes weight; Proposition~1 of~\citep{das2026aip} does not bind \race]\label{prop:quantisation}
Under the one-coin model a report $R_j=c$ shifts the log-odds of $c$ against any alternative by $\lambda_j=\log\frac{(K-1)a_j}{1-a_j}$, and $\operatorname{sign}\lambda_j=\operatorname{sign}(a_j-1/K)$. Consider the gate-aware attacker: it joins a shared lie with probability $p$, otherwise lies independently, and never states the truth. Its pair coherence is $q(p)=p^2+(1-p^2)/(K-1)$, which sweeps $[1/(K-1),1]$. Yet $a_j=0$ and $\lambda_j=-\infty$ for every $p$.
\end{proposition}
Coherence therefore carries no information about the quantity that determines the Bayes weight (\cref{fig:concept}). The original impossibility result concerns rules measurable with respect to per-question coherence. \race's statistic depends on the joint law of $(R_j,Y)$, estimated through the anchored latent model, so it lies outside that class.

\begin{proposition}[History needed to invert correctly]\label{prop:history}
Fix the groups. Let $\hat y$ have error rate $\delta$ on the $n_j$ history questions where peer $j$ was heard, and let $\hat a_j$ be $j$'s agreement with $\hat y$. Then
\[
P\bigl(\operatorname{sign}(\hat a_j-1/K)\neq\operatorname{sign}(a_j-1/K)\bigr)\le 2\exp\!\bigl(-2n_j(|a_j-1/K|-\delta)^2\bigr)
\qquad\text{whenever }|a_j-1/K|>\delta.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$|\hat a_j-\bar a_j|\le\delta$, where $\bar a_j$ is the empirical accuracy. Apply Hoeffding's inequality~\citep{hoeffding1963} to $\bar a_j$.
\end{proof}
Take an always-wrong liar on four-way questions ($|a_j-1/4|=0.25$) and $\delta=0.1$. Then $n_j=80$ gives a bound of $0.055$, and $n_j=160$ gives $0.003$. \Cref{fig:history} measures the realised curve.

\begin{proposition}[Raw agreement confuses competence with dependence]\label{prop:clones}
Take two channels that are conditionally independent given $Y$, with accuracies $a,b$ and symmetric errors. Then $P(R_1{=}R_2)=ab+\frac{(1-a)(1-b)}{K-1}\to1$ as $a,b\to1$, while
\[
P(R_1{=}R_2\mid R_1{\neq}Y\text{ or }R_2{\neq}Y)=\frac{(1-a)(1-b)}{(K-1)(1-ab)}\le\frac1{K-1}.
\]
For replicas of one source, and for a perfectly coordinated bloc, the second quantity equals $1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$P(\text{equal},\ \text{not both correct})=(1-a)(1-b)/(K-1)$ and $P(\text{not both correct})=1-ab\ge(1-a)(1-b)$.
\end{proof}
The same confound explains a refuted recipe in the original ledger: ``maximise effective swarm size'' largely restated roster weakness~\citep{das2026aip}.
