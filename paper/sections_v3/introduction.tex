\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Multi-agent LLM systems increasingly decide by pooling: several agents answer, and each agent combines what it hears. Examples include self-consistency~\citep{wang2023sc}, debate~\citep{du2024debate}, judge panels~\citep{verga2024juries} and agent swarms~\citep{park2023generative}. Pooling fails when some agents are adversarial: compromised by prompt injection~\citep{greshake2023}, fine-tuned to deceive~\citep{hubinger2024sleeper}, or simply instructed to mislead. The classical answer, inherited from Byzantine fault tolerance~\citep{lamport1982,blanchard2017,yin2018}, is to \emph{discard} outliers. That assumes an honest majority and breaks at $f=1/2$.

The ``Liars Are Information'' study~\citep{das2026aip} made a sharp observation: a liar who reliably says $X$ when the truth is not $X$ is not noise. ``This agent says $X$'' is evidence \emph{against} $X$. Its mechanism, \aip, inverts a channel when the channel is more \emph{coherent} with other dissenters than honest agents ever are. The same study then defeated \aip. An adversary that knows the published coherence ceiling coordinates just below it, and no rule measurable with respect to per-question coherence can separate it from honest disagreement. Two further failures were documented. On binary questions coherence is degenerate, and \aip\ scores 0\%. And on open-answer mathematics \aip\ fell below a receiver that simply trusts itself.

\paragraph{This paper.} We argue that the premise was right and the statistic was wrong. What makes a liar informative is not coherence but \emph{dependence on the truth}. An agent that never states the truth leaks it by elimination, whether or not it coordinates. An agent whose answers are independent of the truth leaks nothing, however coherent it is. Dependence on the truth is exactly what latent-truth models of annotator reliability estimate~\citep{dawid1979,zhang2016}. They fail here for a different reason: label-free estimation is identified only up to a relabelling of the truth, and with an adversarial plurality it adopts the liars' labelling. The fix is the one piece of knowledge a receiver always has: \emph{it is itself honest}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\race\ (Receiver-Anchored Channel Estimation).} Each honest receiver fits a one-coin latent-truth model to its own unlabelled history. The fit is anchored on the receiver through initialisation, a prior and a constraint. Replicated channels are tempered by error-conditioned clone detection. The resulting signed, continuous log-odds weight subsumes \aip's TRUST/\allowbreak DISCARD/\allowbreak INVERT gate (\cref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Theory.} (i) Against a receiver that knows the channels, a lie can only add information, and the minimax attack is to be uninformative, so there is no breakdown point below $f=1$ (\cref{thm:silence}). (ii) Label-free estimators must fail at $f\ge1/2$ by label switching; a receiver anchor restores identifiability, and a near-chance anchor provably cannot (\cref{prop:switching}). (iii) \aip's gate is a quantisation of the Bayes weight, and the coherence impossibility does not bind a truth-dependence estimator (\cref{prop:quantisation}). (iv) With three informative channels the anchored estimator is consistent, and its plug-in decision attains the known-channel accuracy (\cref{thm:consistency}). (v) We give sample-complexity and clone-detection results that explain the design (\cref{prop:history,prop:clones}).
\item \textbf{Evaluation.} Over \nWorldsReplay\ replayed worlds on six benchmarks with nine models, fourteen symbolic attacks and four prompt-injected LLM attacks, \race\ holds at every Byzantine fraction up to $0.9$. With real LLM deceivers at $f=0.7$, \race\ scores \llmRaceSeven\% against \llmAipSeven\% for \aip, \llmMajSeven\% for majority vote and \llmSelfSeven\% for the receiver alone. Against the gate-aware attacker that defeats \aip, \race\ scores \zooGateRaceSeven\% against \zooGateAipSeven\%. Four classical crowdsourcing estimators and both Dawid--Skene variants reach at most \crowdClassicalMaxSeven\% at $f=0.7$, where \race\ scores \crowdRaceSeven\% (\cref{sec:crowd}). We report where \race\ loses as prominently: a camouflage attacker built against it, sleepers that violate stationarity, and weak receivers that cannot anchor.
\item \textbf{An information budget for liars.} We compare a known-channel oracle with an oracle that knows who lies and removes them. The gap measures how much information an attack carries, and a negative gap certifies that the attack violates the model. Against independent liars at $f=0.7$, \race\ scores \extRaceIndepSeven\%, above the liar-removal oracle's \extRemovalIndepSeven\%. A rule that only filters liars cannot do that in expectation. Against real LLM deceivers the budget is near zero, and \race\ matches the liar-removal oracle in \removalLlmTies\ of \removalLlmCells\ cells (\cref{sec:budget}).
\item \textbf{A live multi-agent LLM experiment.} Six small open models from six labs answer \nLiveTasks\ questions in honest and covert-saboteur roles, and in a debate condition in which honest agents first see a panel that is half liars (\cref{sec:live}). This is new inference, evaluated after the method was frozen. On MMLU \race\ lifts every honest agent. On binary BoolQ it exposed a misspecification: small models answer with an option bias that a symmetric channel cannot represent. We therefore amended \race\ to use class-conditional channels on binary questions (v3.1, \cref{sec:binary}), re-ran every study, and report the frozen v3.0 alongside it.
\item \textbf{An audit and a repaired, re-derived artifact.} We found that the upstream post-repair recompute was incomplete, found four code defects and a replica-cloning defect, and re-derived every number from the repaired cache (\cref{sec:audit}). All code, data, figures, videos and the claims in this paper are regenerated by one script.
\end{enumerate}
