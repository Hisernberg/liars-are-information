\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

``Liars are information'' is true in a precise sense. A lie is information exactly to the extent that it depends on the truth, and a receiver that knows it is honest can measure that dependence without labels. Replacing a coherence gate with receiver-anchored channel estimation removes the binary collapse and the gate-aware defeat of the prior mechanism. Across all six benchmarks and Byzantine fractions up to $0.9$, it is never significantly worse than a receiver that trusts only itself, with three exceptions: the camouflage and sleeper attacks, and one open-answer cell where the receiver cannot anchor. It also holds up on real LLM deceivers that were not inspected during development. The information budget makes the title measurable. Against independent liars at $f=0.7$, \race\ does better than an oracle that knows who lies and removes them, which no amount of filtering can achieve in expectation. The same analysis says where the idea must stop. An attacker that answers independently of the truth can only silence itself. An attacker whose lies depend on the honest votes, or change over time, attacks the estimator's assumptions rather than its weights. A receiver at chance cannot anchor anything. The live swarm adds another: small models answer binary questions with a fixed option bias, which a symmetric channel model cannot represent. Three directions follow. First, camouflage calls for channel models conditioned on the honest vote pattern; restricting the fit to contested questions (\raceD) is not enough. Second, binary questions call for class-conditional channels, which recovered the live BoolQ loss in our ablation. Third, protocols should record independent answers before agents see each other: on MMLU, debate removed information that pooling needed (\cref{sec:live}).
