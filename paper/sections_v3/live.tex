\section{E8: a live multi-agent LLM swarm}\label{sec:live}

The replay studies reuse cached answers from the original artifact. E8 generates new ones. Six small instruction-tuned models from six labs sit on one panel: Qwen2.5-1.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B, Granite-3.3-2B, OLMo-2-1B, Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma-3-1B. They answer the first \nLiveTasks\ questions of MMLU and BoolQ (120 each) on a CPU, in float32. Every answer is the arg-max of the next-token distribution over the option letters. The question prefix is encoded once, and the key--value cache is branched for each role's instruction, so every role sees exactly the same question. The run started after \race\ was frozen, and nothing about it was inspected before the evaluation below.

\paragraph{Roles.} In round~1 each model answers twice. As an \emph{honest} agent it answers the question. As a \emph{solo} saboteur it is told to work out the answer privately and give a plausible wrong option. In round~2 each model answers twice more. As a \emph{rushing} saboteur it first sees the honest panel's votes and picks the wrong option most likely to mislead them. As a \emph{debating} honest agent it first sees all round-1 votes, half of them from solo saboteurs, and is told only that some panelists may be unreliable. A \emph{colluding} bloc needs no further inference: every colluder adopts the coalition leader's solo lie.

\paragraph{Worlds.} The live answers are evaluated with the same harness as the replay studies. Ten identities are drawn from the six-model roster. For independent answers we cross the Byzantine fraction $f\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7\}$ with four attacks: solo, rushing, colluding LLM saboteurs, and the symbolic coherent bloc. For post-debate answers we use $f=0.5$ with solo and colluding saboteurs. Each setting has five seeds. The HISTORY/VALIDATION/TEST split and all statistics are as in \cref{sec:protocol}.

\paragraph{What the agents did.} Honest accuracy is low and spread widely: \liveMmluHonestMin--\liveMmluHonestMax\% on MMLU and \liveBoolqHonestMin--\liveBoolqHonestMax\% on BoolQ. Instructed saboteurs lie only partly (\cref{fig:live}, top). On MMLU, solo and rushing saboteurs are right \liveMmluSolo\% and \liveMmluRushing\% of the time, against 25\% at chance, and \liveMmluBelowChance\% of the saboteur channels are below chance. On binary BoolQ \emph{none} are: saboteurs are right \liveBoolqSolo\% (solo) and \liveBoolqRushing\% (rushing) of the time. The models do not so much lie as fall back on a fixed option. Granite-3.3-2B as a saboteur says ``yes'' on \liveGraniteLieYes\% of BoolQ questions. SmolLM2-1.7B as an \emph{honest} agent says ``yes'' on \liveSmolYes\% of them (the gold share is \liveBoolqGoldYes\%): it is right on \liveSmolAccYes\% of yes-questions and \liveSmolAccNo\% of no-questions. Channels like these carry information through their asymmetry, which the symmetric one-coin model of \cref{sec:method} cannot represent. In the debate round, honest agents changed their answer on \liveSwitch\% of questions, but only \liveSwitchToLie\% moved to the saboteurs' plurality answer. Their accuracy barely changed (\liveAccIndependent\% before, \liveAccDebate\% after).

\paragraph{MMLU: the thesis holds with weak agents.} With independent answers and LLM saboteurs at $f=0.5$, \race\ scores \liveMmluLlmRaceFive\%. That is above the receiver alone (\liveMmluLlmSelfFive\%), majority vote (\liveMmluLlmMajFive\%), \aip\ (\liveMmluLlmAipFive\%) and label-free Dawid--Skene (\liveMmluLlmDsFive\%). It matches the known-channel oracle (\liveMmluLlmOracleFive\%). At $f=0.7$ the figures are \liveMmluLlmRaceSeven\%, \liveMmluLlmSelfSeven\%, \liveMmluLlmMajSeven\%, \liveMmluLlmAipSeven\% and \liveMmluLlmDsSeven\%. Averaged over LLM saboteurs at $f\in\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}$, every honest receiver gains, by \liveMmluGainMin\ to \liveMmluGainMax\ points. Against the symbolic coherent bloc at $f=0.5$, majority vote falls to \liveMmluCohMajFive\% while \race\ scores \liveMmluCohRaceFive\%.

\paragraph{BoolQ: a misspecification the live data exposed.} Against the coherent bloc, \race\ reads the lie backwards as in the replay studies: \liveBoolqCohRaceFive\% at $f=0.5$, against \liveBoolqCohSelfFive\% alone and \liveBoolqCohMajFive\% for majority vote. Against LLM saboteurs, however, the frozen v3.0 fell \emph{below} the receiver alone: \liveBoolqLlmVthreeFive\% against \liveBoolqLlmSelfFive\% at $f=0.5$, while majority vote reached \liveBoolqLlmMajFive\%. The strongest receiver lost most (Granite-3.3-2B: \liveGraniteSelf\% alone, \liveGraniteVthree\% with v3.0), and the weakest gained, so this is not the weak-anchor failure of \cref{sec:limits}. The cause is the option bias above. A channel that says ``yes'' regardless of the truth has one-coin accuracy close to $1/2$, so its information sits in its asymmetry, which the one-coin model discards. Class-conditional channels model that asymmetry. With them (v3.1, \cref{sec:binary}), \race\ scores \liveBoolqLlmRaceFive\% at $f=0.5$ and \liveBoolqLlmRaceSeven\% at $f=0.7$ (majority: \liveBoolqLlmMajSeven\%), and the strongest receiver scores \liveGraniteRace\%. We adopted this rule after seeing these data, so this is not a confirmation of v3.1. On replayed BoolQ, v3.1 is never significantly worse than v3.0: \binaryReplayWins\ of \binaryReplayCells\ cells favour v3.1 and \binaryReplayLosses\ favour v3.0 (\cref{app:binary}). Each world has only \nLiveTestTasks\ test questions, so few per-cell differences are significant. Across the \liveCellsPerBaseline\ cells per baseline, \race\ (v3.1) loses \liveLossesSelf\ to the receiver alone, \liveLossesMaj\ to majority vote, \liveLossesAip\ to \aip\ and \liveLossesDs\ to label-free Dawid--Skene. It wins \liveWinsSelf, \liveWinsMaj, \liveWinsAip\ and \liveWinsDs\ cells respectively.

\paragraph{Debate: it depends on whether the saboteurs carry signal.} We compare post-debate with independent answers on the matched attacks (solo and colluding saboteurs, $f=0.5$). On MMLU, debate lowers \race\ from \liveIndMmluRace\% to \liveDebMmluRace\%, majority vote from \liveIndMmluMaj\% to \liveDebMmluMaj\% and the receiver alone from \liveIndMmluSelf\% to \liveDebMmluSelf\%. There the saboteurs' answers are informative (half of them below chance), and after debate the honest answers are no longer independent draws, so a receiver-local estimator has less to work with. On BoolQ the saboteurs carry almost no signal, and debate \emph{helps}: \race\ rises from \liveIndBoolqRace\% to \liveDebBoolqRace\% and majority vote from \liveIndBoolqMaj\% to \liveDebBoolqMaj\%. The safe protocol is to record each agent's independent answer before any discussion. Pooling can then use the independent answers, the post-debate answers, or both.
