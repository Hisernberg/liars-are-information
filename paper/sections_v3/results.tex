\subsection{E1: no breakdown point against a coherent bloc}
\Cref{fig:main} is the headline. Averaged over the six benchmarks at $f=0.5$, \race\ reaches \mainRaceFive\% against \mainAipFive\% for \aip, \mainMajFive\% for majority vote, \mainDsFive\% for label-free Dawid--Skene and \mainSelfFive\% for the receiver alone. At $f=0.9$ the figures are \mainRaceNine\%, \mainAipNine\%, \mainMajNine\%, \mainDsNine\% and \mainSelfNine\%. The mean accuracy of majority vote and label-free Dawid--Skene first falls below half the receiver's at $f=\breakdownMaj$ and $f=\breakdownDs$. For \aip\ this happens at $f=\breakdownAip$, and for \race\ at \breakdownRace\ of the tested fractions. The known-channel oracle bounds the remaining headroom at \mainOracleFive\% ($f=0.5$).

The binary case, which \aip\ cannot handle, is where inversion is most valuable. On BoolQ at $f=0.7$, \race\ scores \mainBoolqRaceSeven\% (\aip: \mainBoolqAipSeven\%, receiver: \mainBoolqSelfSeven\%). A bloc that always says the opposite is a perfect oracle once it is read backwards. With all agents honest ($f=0$), \race\ costs nothing: \mainRaceZero\% against \mainAipZero\% for \aip\ and \mainMajZero\% for majority. Across all $6\times10$ cells (Holm-corrected Wilcoxon, \cref{tab:wtl}), \race\ is significantly better than \aip\ in \mainWinsAip\ cells and worse in \mainLossesAip. Against majority it wins \mainWinsMaj\ cells and loses \mainLossesMaj. Against the receiver alone it wins \mainWinsSelf\ and never loses. Every significant loss is on MATH-500 at $f\le0.3$, the weak-anchor regime analysed in \cref{sec:limits}. Clone tempering has a price against a \emph{perfectly} coherent bloc. Counting the bloc's identical vetoes separately happens to help there (\mainRaceNocloneFive\% without tempering at $f=0.5$), but the tempered rule is the one that wins on the confirmation sets (\cref{sec:llm}).

\subsection{E2: the attack zoo and the gate-aware adversary}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig2_attack_zoo.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{E2.} Accuracy minus the receiver alone (percentage points), averaged over six benchmarks and three seeds, for 14 attack settings at three Byzantine fractions. Blue: pooling helps. Red: pooling hurts.}\label{fig:zoo}
\end{figure}
\Cref{fig:zoo} organises the attacks by the assumption they target. Three findings stand out.

\emph{Coherence gates are blind to incoherent liars.} Against the gate-aware attacker at $f=0.7$ (coordination $p\le0.5$, i.e.\ below \aip's published ceiling), \aip\ scores \zooGateAipSeven\% and its soft gate \zooGateSoftSeven\%, while \race\ scores \zooGateRaceSeven\% (\cref{fig:gate}). Independent liars, who never coordinate at all, give \zooIndepAipSeven\% versus \zooIndepRaceSeven\%. This is \cref{prop:quantisation} in data. The attacker's coherence is tunable, but its dependence on the truth is not.

\emph{The minimax attack costs little.} Uninformative liars are the minimax strategy of \cref{thm:silence}. Against them \race\ stays at \zooUninfRaceSeven\% at $f=0.7$, close to the honest-only oracle (\zooUninfOracleSeven\%) and above the receiver alone (\zooUninfSelfSeven\%).

\emph{\race's own failure modes.} Camouflage (\cref{sec:limits}) is the one stationary attack that pushes \race\ below the receiver at $f=0.7$ (\zooCamoRaceSeven\% vs \zooCamoSelfSeven\%). The sleeper defeats every static fit.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig3_gate_aware.pdf}
\caption{The gate-aware adversary. The upper axis shows the attacker's pair coherence $q(p)$. \aip\ is defeated exactly in the sub-ceiling band. \race\ is nearly flat in $p$ (within about 3.5 points at $f=0.7$), because the attacker's accuracy, not its coherence, sets the weight.}\label{fig:gate}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Adaptive attackers.} For every defender, benchmark, $f$ and seed we let the attacker pick, on VALIDATION, whichever stationary zoo attack hurts that defender most, and score TEST under that choice (\cref{fig:br}). Under its own best response, \race\ holds \brRaceFive\% at $f=0.5$ and \brRaceSeven\% at $f=0.7$. The corresponding figures are \brAipFive\% and \brAipSeven\% for \aip, \brMajFive\% and \brMajSeven\% for majority, and \brSelfFive\% and \brSelfSeven\% for the receiver alone. Including the sleeper, every static method fails at $f\ge0.5$. \Cref{sec:online} addresses this with online refitting.

\subsection{E3: replayed LLM deceivers (confirmation set)}\label{sec:llm}
The four prompt-injected attacks of the original study were written by real LLMs. Their lies are neither perfectly wrong nor perfectly coordinated. This set was not inspected before the method was frozen. \race\ is above every non-\race\ method at every $f$, and within $0.1$ points of its own best variant (\cref{fig:llm}). At $f=0.7$ it scores \llmRaceSeven\%, against \llmAipSeven\% for \aip, \llmMajSeven\% for majority, \llmDsSeven\% for Dawid--Skene and \llmSelfSeven\% for the receiver alone. That is within \llmOracleSeven\% of the known-channel oracle and above the honest-only oracle (\llmHonestOracleSeven\%), because inverting the liars adds information that honest agents alone do not have. Across \llmCells\ Holm-corrected comparisons (5 baselines $\times$ 6 benchmarks $\times$ 4 prompts $\times$ 4 fractions), \race\ has \llmWins\ significant wins and \llmLosses\ losses. The confirmation result proper is that of the frozen v3.0 rule, which differs from v3.1 only on BoolQ: \llmWinsVthree\ wins and \llmLossesVthree\ losses. Both superseded variants of the clone rule score lower on this set (\llmRaceRawSeven\% and \llmRaceNocloneSeven\% at $f=0.7$). This is independent support for the one post-hoc design change (\cref{app:devlog}).

\subsection{E7: does RACE estimate the channels?}
\race's estimates are label-free. We compared them with each peer's true accuracy on HISTORY, which only the evaluator can compute, over \chanN\ receiver--peer channels spanning seven attacks, four fractions and six benchmarks (\cref{fig:chan}). The correlation is $r=\chanCorr$ and the mean absolute error is \chanMae. \race\ inverts \chanByzInvRace\% of Byzantine channels, against \chanByzInvAip\% for \aip. It falsely inverts \chanHonInvRace\% of honest channels (\aip: \chanHonInvAip\%). The Byzantine channels \race\ trusts (\chanByzTrustRace\%) are liars that tell the truth often enough to be positively informative, such as partial and camouflage attackers and LLM liars that fail to lie.

For receivers above 50\%, \race\ ends more than 5 points worse than trusting themselves in only \riskAboveRace\% of cases. The rates are \riskAboveAip\% for \aip\ and \riskAboveMaj\% for majority. For receivers at or below 50\%, the weak-anchor regime of \cref{prop:switching}, the rate rises to \riskBelowRace\%.

\subsection{E5: how much history?}
\race\ needs little history (\cref{fig:history}). Averaged over attacks and benchmarks at $f=0.5$, it reaches \histRaceFive\% with 5 history questions and \histRaceTwoZero\% with 20. \aip\ reaches \histAipFive\% and \histAipTwoZero\%: its significance test has a minimum-support floor. This matches \cref{prop:history}. An always-wrong liar is far from chance, so few joint observations separate it.
