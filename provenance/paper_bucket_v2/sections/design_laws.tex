\section{Swarm-Level Design Laws}
\label{sec:designlaws}

Three properties of an honest swarm have to be measured before any defence is
evaluated, because all three appear inside the mechanism as constants and all
three constrain what a swarm can do at all. Each turns out to be a design law
with a counter-intuitive edge: correlated failure is the default rather than the
exception, decorrelation does not buy accuracy, and what an adversary can
coordinate on is a property of the answer space rather than of the adversary.
The first of the three is established prior art in the evaluation-panel
setting~\cite{judgecorr,panelcorr,hivemind}; we confirm it in an adversarial
swarm and extend it, and we are explicit below about which parts are which.

Read together they make one claim: \emph{an LLM swarm's ceiling on honest error
is correlation, not competence}, which leaves adversarial coherence as the only
structure a swarm can exploit that its honest members do not already share.

\subsection{Law 1: correlated failure is the default}

\textbf{This law is not ours to claim.} That LLM errors are correlated across
models is established by Kim et al.~\cite{judgecorr}, that the correlation costs
a nine-judge panel all but two effective votes by Kohli~\cite{panelcorr}, and the
underlying output homogeneity by Jiang et al.~\cite{hivemind}. What follows
confirms the ceiling in an adversarial swarm rather than an evaluation panel, and
extends it in two ways the adversarial setting forces.

\emph{The extension that matters for deployment} is which pair is compared. A
panel is assembled from distinct models; a deployed swarm is more likely built by
replicating one, and replication is the more correlated case: error correlation
between two agents running the \emph{same} model at different sampling
temperatures is $\numWithinModelPhi$ against $\numCrossModelPhi$ between
different models, a factor of $\numPhiRatio$. The full pairwise matrix is in the
artifact. Replicating a model does not replicate an independent opinion, and it
is worse than mixing by more than half again. \textbf{[C1]}

\emph{The extension that matters for a defence} is that the quantity becomes
predictive. The multiclass attenuation law (Section~\ref{sec:theory}) is exact at
$C=2$ and validated against that exactness, so the correlation structure is
estimated blind at run time without labels --- which is what lets it serve as the
constant an inversion gate fires against.

In agent counts: a homogeneous swarm of $\numAgents$ agents is worth
$\numNeffHomMin$--$\numNeffHomMax$ independent agents, mean $\numNeffHomMean$;
the best two-model mixture reaches $\numNeffHetMax$. A ten-agent homogeneous
swarm is closer to one epistemic agent than to ten --- the swarm analogue of nine
judges being worth two votes. \textbf{[C2]}

\textbf{A basis restriction.} Only GSM8K carries a measured within-model
correlation, since only there did we sample the same model twice; elsewhere the
mixed-swarm figures use cross-model correlation alone and are \emph{upper bounds}
on $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The largest, $\numNeffUpperBoundMax$, comes from a
different estimator on an excluded benchmark and is not comparable.

\subsection{Law 2: decorrelation does not buy accuracy}

The obvious reading of Law 1 is that a practitioner should maximise
$N_{\mathrm{eff}}$. We tested that directly and it is wrong.

For every $\numCompSubsetsThree$ three-model and $\numCompSubsetsFive$
five-model subset we build a $\numAgents$-agent swarm split as evenly as
possible, average $\rho$ over the real pair census, and compute both
$N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and plurality accuracy over the cached answers
(Table~\ref{tab:composition}). Every distinct assignment of a surplus agent is
enumerated and averaged, since the heavier model wins any three-way disagreement
by weight alone.

$N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ turns out to be close to a restatement of roster weakness ---
Spearman $\numCompNeffVsAccMean$ against mean roster accuracy, with every
diversity-flavoured predictor losing its association once the mean is partialled
out --- and, more usefully, \textbf{it is negatively associated with swarm
accuracy}: $\numCompAccVsNeff$ at $k=3$, against $\numCompAccVsAccMean$ for mean
accuracy. Maximising effective swarm size selects \emph{against} what a
practitioner wants. What survives partialling is accuracy \emph{spread}, partial
Spearman about $+0.7$: among rosters of equal average strength, prefer unequal
members --- a second-order effect on top of ``use accurate models''.

\textbf{Family diversity cannot be assessed here}: all $\numModels$ models come
from $\numModels$ labs, so every $k$-subset contains exactly $k$ lineages and the
predictor is constant by construction --- a limitation of the roster, not
evidence that lineage is irrelevant.

Composition choice clears the floor on the three benchmarks with headroom and not
on the three saturated ones; but on \emph{no} benchmark does the best composition
beat its own strongest single member detectably (largest
$\numCompBestGainMedqa$), and on MATH-500 every three-model composition is
\emph{worse} than its best member by at least $\numCompBestGainMathfive$.

This is the negative result we would keep if we could keep only one. With Law 1
and the honest attractors it says an LLM swarm's ceiling on honest error is
\textbf{correlation, not competence}: the agents fail together, into the same
wrong answers, and adding more does not help because what is added is not
independent. We searched every three- and five-model subset of the roster for an
exception and found none.

The consequence is direct: if honest pooling is capped, the only channel through
which a swarm exceeds its best member is the structure honest agents do
\emph{not} share --- adversarial coherence. That is what threshold-gated
inversion spends, which is why the positive result here is about robustness
rather than accuracy, and why an adversary that declines to be coherent defeats
the mechanism by giving it nothing to spend. \textbf{[S1]}

\subsection{Law 3: coordinatability is a property of the answer space}

An adversary is exploitable only if it can be \emph{coherent}, which requires a
lie rule its members compute identically from the question alone.

We expected coordinatability to collapse on closed label spaces, reasoning that
four-way multiple choice offers no shared rule for choosing \emph{which} wrong
option to name. \textbf{The measurement runs the other way}: mean adversarial
coherence is \emph{highest} on the most closed space --- $\numQhatBinaryMean$
binary, $\numQhatMcMean$ four-way multiple choice, against
$\numQhatOpenTextMean$ open text. A smaller answer space makes accidental
agreement easier, not harder.

What does collapse is the quantity a gate needs: the adversary's \emph{margin
over honest agreement}. On four-way multiple choice the negation attack reaches
$\hat q = \numMmluNegQhat$ against an honest receiver-conditioned floor of
$\numMmluHonestFloor$, and no attack's interval clears that floor. On
open-numeric answers the same attack reaches $\numGsmkNegQhat$ against a floor of
$\numGsmkHonestFloor$ and does clear it. On a closed space there is no coherent
channel \emph{relative to honest behaviour} for a gate to license inversion on.
\textbf{[G2']}

\subsection{The constant the gate is calibrated on}

The calibration constant is the rate at which two honest agents, both wrong, are
wrong the \emph{same} way. On MMLU it is $\numHonestQMmlu$ against a chance level
of $\numMmluChanceQ$; on MATH-500, where the answer space is effectively open, it
is $\numHonestQMathfive$. Honest agents are three to four times more likely to
collide on a wrong answer than chance predicts, and that is the entire reason a
coherence-triggered inversion rule needs a gate at all. \textbf{[C3]}

\textbf{Power, stated plainly.} These are per-pair estimates conditioned on both
agents being wrong, so the sample size is jointly-wrong items, not tasks. On
MATH-500 all twenty-one pairs clear the $n \geq 30$ threshold; on MedQA eight do,
on MMLU one. The MMLU figure is reported because the gate uses it, and
Figure~\ref{fig:evasion} draws underpowered pairs separately for that reason.

\subsection{Law 4: the broadcast contract decides what a receiver can believe}
\label{sec:protocol}

What an agent is obliged to broadcast is a security decision, not an interface
detail. Every confidence-weighted defence in the literature reads a number the
sender supplies; each broadcast here carries two, the mean token logprob over the
answer span and a number the model was asked to state. A prompt-injected agent
can assert any self-report but cannot easily inflate the logprob of a span it did
not generate.

As a predictor of its own correctness the logprob surface reaches ROC AUC
$\numAucLogprob$ and the self-report $\numAucSelfReport$, a gap of $\numAucGap$,
over the $\numAucCellsPowered$ of $\numAucCellsTotal$ frozen-roster cells clearing
the power floor (R2: at least thirty wrong answers). The weak-tier arm, reported
separately, shows the same ordering.

The gap is not the argument --- it is real but modest. The argument is what
happens under attack. The \texttt{falsified\_confidence} attack inverts the
self-report and leaves answer and logprob byte-identical, so anything it changes
is attributable to that channel. It drives self-report discrimination from
$\numAucSelfReport$ to $\numAucSelfReportFalsified$ --- \emph{below chance}, on
$\numAucBelowChanceCells$ of $\numAucCellsPowered$ cells. A receiver weighting on
that surface is not merely uninformed, it is anti-informed. The logprob surface is
unmoved exactly: AIP's accuracy under this attack differs from its control by
$\numFalsifiedGsmkAip$ on GSM8K and $\numFalsifiedMmluAip$ on MMLU --- an
implementation identity rather than an estimate below the floor, since the gate
never reads the self-report. \textbf{[F1, P1]}

A third failure mode needs no adversary: reasoning models frequently emit no
parseable number on multiple choice, and the self-report is missing on up to
$\numSelfReportMissingMax$ of rows in the worst powered cell. We report that
rather than prompting until compliance improves, because the non-compliance is
the finding --- a field a well-behaved agent may omit and a misbehaving one may
fabricate is a weaker commitment than one derived from tokens already emitted.
\textbf{Broadcast both surfaces and weight on the logprob}: the one
recommendation here that the gate-aware adversary does not reach, because it
rests on no coherence statistic.

\subsection{Blind estimator validation}

The multiclass law beats the binary law wherever the answer space is not binary
(MATH-500 $\numMaeMathfiveBinary \to \numMaeMathfiveMc$ mean absolute error,
GSM8K $\numMaeGsmkBinary \to \numMaeGsmkMc$), and on BoolQ, where $C=2$ makes the
binary law exact by construction, it recovers the same answer,
$\numMaeBoolqMc$ --- the control condition for the estimator. \textbf{[C4]} The
exception is MMLU, where it does \emph{not} beat the binary law
($\numMaeMmluBinary$ against $\numMaeMmluMc$); we have no account of why and do
not claim the blind estimator is validated there.
