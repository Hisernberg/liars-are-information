\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A decentralized swarm of language-model agents answers a question by
broadcasting individual answers and locally aggregating what it hears. When a
fraction $f$ of the agents is Byzantine, the aggregation rule decides whether the
swarm survives, and essentially every rule in the literature survives by
\emph{discarding}: Krum selects the contribution closest to its
neighbours~\cite{blanchard2017krum}, coordinate-wise median and trimmed mean
suppress outlying coordinates~\cite{yin2018trimmed}, confidence weighting
down-weights low-confidence peers, filter-and-refine schemes drop contributors
whose history looks unreliable. The shared premise is that adversarial input is
contamination, and it yields the familiar ceiling: once adversaries hold the
plurality, a rule that treats consensus as evidence follows them.

\subsection{Why a swarm needs an adversary to be worth pooling}

Before defending a swarm it is worth asking what pooling honest agents buys, and
the answer we measure is: very little. Correlated error already caps evaluation
panels --- nine LLM judges are worth about two effective
votes~\cite{judgecorr,panelcorr}, and open-ended model output is narrower than
model count suggests~\cite{hivemind}. We confirm that ceiling in an adversarial
swarm and extend it to the case a deployment actually builds: replicating one
model gives within-model error correlation $\numWithinModelPhi$ against
$\numCrossModelPhi$ across families. Over every three-model subset of our roster,
no composition beats its own strongest single member by a detectable margin, and
on MATH-500 every composition is \emph{worse} by at least
$\numCompBestGainMathfive$; maximising effective swarm size correlates with swarm
accuracy at $\numCompAccVsNeff$, the wrong sign.

\textbf{An LLM swarm's ceiling on honest error is correlation, not competence.}
The consequence is sharp: the only structure in the broadcast set that honest
agents do not already share is \emph{adversarial} coherence. A disciplined liar
is the one genuinely informative contributor a swarm has.

\subsection{Liars are information}

An adversary that lies \emph{coherently} --- reporting the same wrong answer as
its collaborators, task after task --- is not injecting noise. It is operating a
channel whose sign is recoverable: if a bloc reliably says $X$ when the truth is
not $X$, then ``this bloc says $X$'' is evidence \emph{against} $X$. Discarding
it throws that away; inverting it converts the adversary into a contributor.

Measuring coherence requires no labels. Writing $e_i$ for an agent's error rate
and $q_{ij}$ for the probability that two agents who are both wrong are wrong
\emph{in the same way}, agreement obeys a generalized attenuation law
$m_{ij} = (1-e_i)(1-e_j) + e_i e_j q_{ij}$, of which the binary case used in
prior work is the instance $q_{ij}\equiv1$. The coincidence term is not a
nuisance parameter; it is the inversion signal.

The obstacle is that honest agents are not idiosyncratic either --- we measure
honest coincidence at $\numHonestQMmlu$ on four-way multiple choice against a
$\numMmluChanceQ$ chance level --- so a rule triggered by coherence alone inverts
honest agents precisely on the questions they find hardest. Our gate therefore
fires only when a channel's coherence exceeds a calibrated honest ceiling
\emph{and} it agrees with the receiver less than the attenuation law permits.
Both tests are anchored on the receiver's own answer, which is what keeps them
meaningful past $f=0.5$.

\subsection{The paper's turn}

The mechanism works, and then it is defeated. An adversary that knows the gate
exists does not have to beat the threshold --- it can stay under it. We construct
one, show it reduces AIP to $\numEvasionAccMedqa$ at the exact tie, and prove
that no rule computed from per-question coherence separates it from honest
independent disagreement, because at its optimum it is \emph{less} coherent than
honest agents are. Of the pre-registered mitigations, soft gating partly works
and threshold randomisation does nothing measurable.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mechanism.} A local three-way channel decision (invert / trust /
      discard) on a calibrated honest-coincidence threshold, with a windowed
      variant for non-stationary adversaries.
\item \textbf{Theory.} A multiclass attenuation law with its identifiability
      conditions; \emph{coordinatability}; and Proposition~1, a total-variation
      limit showing a residual attack at or below chance coherence is invisible
      to \emph{every} rule measurable with respect to per-question coherence.
\item \textbf{Design laws: confirming and extending the correlation ceiling.}
      The ceiling is established for evaluation
      panels~\cite{judgecorr,panelcorr,hivemind}; we confirm it under adversarial
      conditions and extend it three ways --- to replicated models, from an
      observation to a law estimable blind at run time, and from a measurement
      caveat to the premise of a defence.
\item \textbf{Protocol.} Of the two confidence surfaces an agent can broadcast,
      the logprob-derived one discriminates better (AUC $\numAucLogprob$ against
      $\numAucSelfReport$) and cannot be authored by the sender, whose falsified
      self-report drops to $\numAucSelfReportFalsified$, below chance. This is
      the one recommendation here the gate-aware adversary does not reach.
\item \textbf{Evaluation, including what failed.} $\numAttackClasses$ attack
      classes, a pre-registered mitigation study with criteria committed before
      the runs, and a cost table showing the gate is
      $\numCostGateOverheadPct$\% of the aggregation cost it belongs to. Ten
      claims this study set out to confirm were refuted by its own data and are
      reported at the same length as the confirmations.
\end{itemize}

Every effect is compared against a resampling floor: $\numFloorGsmk$ on GSM8K,
$\numFloorWorst$ on the $\numTasksMin$-task benchmarks. An effect below its
cell's floor is \textbf{undetectable at this scale}, never ``small'' or ``no
effect''.
