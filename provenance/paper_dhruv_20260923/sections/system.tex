\section{System, Threat Model, and the Gate}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Swarm and broadcast model}

$N = \numAgents$ agents answer the same task independently. Each broadcasts three
things: an answer, a confidence derived from the mean token logprob over the
answer span, and a separately elicited self-reported confidence. There is no
central aggregator and no shared state; every agent computes
$\mathrm{aggregate}(\text{observations}, \text{self})$ locally, and the ``swarm
answer'' we report is the answer a receiver holds after aggregating.

Topology is complete by default, with ring and $k$-nearest variants; partial
observability $p_{obs} \in \{1, 0.5, \numPobsQuarter, \numPobsTenth\}$ thins
which broadcasts a receiver hears, and it rather than the Byzantine fraction is
the binding practical constraint (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). A receiver's
own vote is a confound for every weighted rule, so all comparisons run at matched
self-vote share; the audit establishing that it is a real confound, and where it
is not controlled, is in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}. \textbf{[M2a, M2b]}

\subsection{Threat model}

The Byzantine fraction ranges over $f \in \{0, \ldots, 0.7\}$. Adversaries may
collude, may know the defence in full, and in Section~\ref{sec:gateaware} are
given the published calibration constants; they cannot forge identities and
cannot observe ground truth.

Three deployability constraints apply to every method we are willing to call a
defence: no oracle knowledge of $f$, no labels at run time, and no trusted third
party. Krum, Multi-Krum and trimmed mean are handed the true Byzantine count and
are therefore marked \emph{oracle} and reported separately. This is not a
rhetorical exclusion. Those methods win on two benchmarks under the real-LLM
adversary---multi-Krum reads $\numDiscardOracleMedqaFseven$ on MedQA where AIP
reads less---and any claim that AIP dominates the discard family is a claim about
the deployable subset only. \textbf{[H2'']}

\subsection{Attack classes}

$\numAttackClasses$ attack classes are evaluated. Four are generated by
prompt-injected LLM adversaries and require inference:
\texttt{always\_wrong} (assert a wrong answer), \texttt{semantic\_negation} (a
structured lie computed by a rule the bloc shares), \texttt{semantic\_hallucination}
(a fluent but idiosyncratic wrong answer), and \texttt{rushing} (condition on
cached honest broadcasts and answer first).

Three are exact offline transforms of a cached adversarial run, which is what
makes them clean rather than cheap. \texttt{falsified\_confidence} inverts the
self-reported confidence and leaves the answer and the logprob byte-identical, so
any difference it produces is attributable to the self-report channel and to
nothing else. \texttt{burst} schedules the coherent-lie cache against noise in
alternating epochs. The adaptive bandit adversary chooses among arms across
epochs according to realised payoff.

Invertibility tracks measured channel coherence, which is the paper's central
diagnostic. On GSM8K, negation reaches $\hat q = \numAttackQNegation$ and is
exploitable; \texttt{rushing} ($\numAttackQRushing$) and \texttt{always\_wrong}
($\numAttackQAlwaysWrong$) sit below the honest ceiling and are not; and
\texttt{semantic\_hallucination} ($\numAttackQHallucination$) carries no channel
at all despite being the most damaging attack in the set. An attack with no
coherent channel is not exploitable however damaging it is. \textbf{[I1, G2']}


The seventh class, an adaptive bandit adversary, has no fixed coherence: it
chooses among the arms above across epochs.

\subsection{The three-way channel decision}

For each peer a receiver makes one of three decisions: \textsc{invert},
\textsc{trust}, or \textsc{discard}.

\textsc{Invert} requires \emph{two} conditions. The channel's coherence must be
significantly above the calibrated honest ceiling for the answer space (one-sided
exact binomial test against a derived minimum-support floor), \emph{and} the
channel must agree with the receiver less than the attenuation law permits for an
honest peer of the estimated error rate. The first test alone is what a naive
mechanism uses and it fails, because honest agents cohere. Both are anchored on
the receiver's own answer rather than the plurality, which is why the mechanism
survives past $f=0.5$: a plurality-anchored statistic has already converged on
the lie there. \textbf{[G1]}

An inverted channel is not merely dropped. It contributes signed informativeness:
weight $-w$ to the answer it broadcast and $+w/(K-1)$ to each of the $K-1$
alternatives, where $w$ scales with the channel's estimated capacity. This is the
step that converts an adversary into a contributor, and the ablation in
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} isolates its contribution by removing it.

\subsection{Windowed statistics for non-stationary adversaries}

Pooled channel history averages a burst adversary's coherent and noisy regimes
into a channel that clears neither threshold, so the pooled gate trusts every
adversary. A sliding window of $W$ tasks, together with a regime-change flag,
restores the decision; a flagged channel is \emph{demoted to discard} and never
inverted, because a channel whose regime just changed is the one case where the
estimated sign is least trustworthy. \textbf{[B1]}

\subsection{Unfakeable confidence}

The two confidence surfaces are cached separately for a reason. A prompt-injected
agent can assert any self-reported confidence it likes; it cannot easily inflate
the mean token logprob of a span it did not generate. AIP reads the logprob
channel. Section~\ref{sec:designlaws} (Law~4) tests this directly: inverting the self-report
moves AIP by exactly zero and moves a self-report-reading baseline by up to
$\numFalsifiedMmluSelfreport$. \textbf{[F1]}
