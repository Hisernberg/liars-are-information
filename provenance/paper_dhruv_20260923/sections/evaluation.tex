\section{Evaluation Under a Symbolic Adversary}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Setup}

The frozen roster is $\numModels$ open-weight models spanning $\numParamsMinB$B
to $\numParamsMaxB$B from seven lineages, plus a separately reported
$\numModelsWeakArm$-model weak-tier arm never pooled with it. Of
$\numBenchmarks$ benchmarks, $\numHeadlineBenchmarks$ are headline-eligible after
excluding cells whose honest accuracy leaves no headroom; each carries
$\numTasksMin$--$\numTasksMax$ frozen tasks and $\numBootstrapResamples$
bootstrap resamples. Accuracy spans $\numModelAccMin$--$\numModelAccMax$, so no
cell is at ceiling or floor.

This section reports the symbolic adversary---a bloc that emits a designated
wrong answer with a specified coherence; the prompt-injected adversaries follow
below, and the two regimes disagree in ways that matter.

\subsection{The headline, and where it does not hold}

Against the symbolic coherent adversary AIP-gated holds
$\numGsmkAipMin$--$\numGsmkAipMax$ on GSM8K across the whole Byzantine range, a
spread of $\numGsmkAipSpread$, and $\numMmluAipMin$--$\numMmluAipMax$ on MMLU, a
spread of $\numMmluAipSpread$. Both spreads are below their cells' measurement
floors, so what the data supports is that no change was detected---not that
accuracy is provably constant. Every deployable discard baseline reads
$\numDiscardCollapseFseven$ at $f=0.7$ in the same configuration
(Figure~\ref{fig:money}).

\textbf{Refuted: accuracy is not flat in $f$ in general.} On MATH-500 the same
mechanism spans $\numMathfiveAipMin$--$\numMathfiveAipMax$, a spread of
$\numMathfiveAipSpread$, falling to $\numMathfiveAipFseven$ at $f=0.7$ from
$\numMathfiveAipFzero$ at $f=0$. Under real-LLM adversaries the fall is present
on four of six benchmarks: $\numHoneFallBoolq$ on BoolQ, $\numHoneFallMedqa$ on
MedQA, $\numHoneFallMmlu$ on MMLU and $\numHoneFallArc$ on ARC, all at least
three times the floor. Only GSM8K ($\numHoneFallGsmk$) and MATH-500
($\numHoneFallMathfive$) are undetectable. The claim this project set out to make
was invariance in $f$; the claim the data supports is invariance on two
benchmarks under one adversary, and a large, ordered degradation elsewhere.
\textbf{[H1]}

\subsection{The discard family: three separate statements}

``Every discard baseline reaches zero'' is true only under conditions that must be
stated, and false under two of them. \textbf{(i) Symbolic adversary, deployable
methods}: true, and exactly --- $\numDiscardCollapseFseven$ on all
$\numBenchmarks$ benchmarks at $f=0.7$. \textbf{(ii) Oracle methods, either
regime}: \emph{false} --- Krum, handed the true Byzantine count, holds
$\numDiscardOracleBoolqFseven$ on BoolQ and $\numDiscardOracleMedqaFseven$ on
MedQA, and multi-Krum beats AIP under the real-LLM adversary on both.
\textbf{(iii) Real-LLM adversary}: \emph{false} --- nothing reaches zero,
deployable discard spans $\numRealDiscardMinFseven$ to
$\numRealDiscardMaxFseven$, and AIP separates on four of six
($\numRealSepGsmkFseven$ GSM8K, $\numRealSepMathfiveFseven$ MATH-500,
$\numRealSepArcFseven$ ARC, $\numRealSepMmluFseven$ MMLU), is undetectable on
MedQA ($\numRealSepMedqaFseven$), and \textbf{loses to plain majority vote on
BoolQ by $\numRealSepBoolqFseven$}. \textbf{[H2', H2'', H2''']}

BoolQ is the informative failure: with $C=2$ every wrong answer is the same wrong
answer, so adversarial coherence is unity by construction and carries no
information. The gate fires on a channel whose sign it cannot use, and majority
vote does better by ignoring it --- the coordinatability argument arriving as a
measured cost.

\subsection{The invertibility spectrum}

Inversion gain tracks measured channel coherence, the mechanism's central
prediction: negation reaches $\hat q = \numGsmkNegationQ$ and gains
$\numGsmkNegationGain$, hallucination reaches $\numGsmkHallucinationQ$ and gains
$\numGsmkHallucinationGain$. \textbf{Honest negative result}: semantic
hallucination is fluent and highly effective as an attack and AIP can do nothing
about it --- with no coherent channel there is no sign to recover and the
mechanism degrades to discard level. An adversary that simply refuses to
coordinate is a complete defence against inversion, at the cost of being a weaker
attack. \textbf{[I2]}


\subsection{Why the gate is necessary}

Ablating inversion --- replacing the three-way decision with trust-or-discard ---
isolates what it contributes: at $f=0.7$ the gain is $\numInversionGainFseven$,
at $f=0$ the cost is $\numInversionCostFzero$, far below the floor. Inversion is
close to free when idle and large when engaged, which is the asymmetry the
mechanism is built for. \textbf{[I1, I2]} The gate keeps the first half true:
across every configuration tested it inverts at most $\numHonestInvertedMax$\% of
honest channels while inverting $\numAdversaryInvertedMean$\% of adversarial
channels at $f \geq 0.5$. \textbf{[G1]}


\subsection{Two results that did not go our way}

\textbf{The gain is not independent of honest competence.} We predicted inversion
gain is a property of the adversarial channel, and that a weaker swarm would gain
\emph{more}, having more honest error to correct. On a matched benchmark set the
weak-tier arm reads $\numInversionGainWeak$ against the roster's
$\numInversionGainFseven$, a difference of $\numInversionGainCompetenceDiff$ ---
above the floor, lower on all three benchmarks individually, and opposite to the
prediction. The comparison that originally supported the claim pooled the weak
arm's six benchmarks ($\numInversionGainWeakAllBenches$) against the roster's
three; correcting it reverses the conclusion. \textbf{[I3]}

\textbf{Whether the gain scales with swarm size is undetectable.} At
$N=\numAgents$, $20$ and $50$ the gain reads $\numInversionGainNTen$,
$\numInversionGainNTwenty$ and $\numInversionGainNFifty$ --- a range of
$\numInversionGainNRange$ against a $\numFloorWorst$ floor. \emph{Neither} growth
nor invariance in $N$ is supported; the experiment was underpowered to separate
them, and we report it as underpowered rather than as evidence for the flat
reading its point estimates suggest. \textbf{[N1]}

\subsection{Real-LLM adversaries}

The symbolic adversary above is a control: its coherence is set, not measured.
Prompt-injected models are why the discard family does not collapse to zero and
why AIP loses on BoolQ.

\textbf{Non-stationary.} A burst adversary alternates coherent and noisy epochs.
Pooled history averages the two into a channel clearing neither threshold and the
pooled gate falls to $\numBurstStatic$ on GSM8K; sliding-window statistics recover
$\numBurstWindowTwenty$ at $W=20$, a gain of $\numBurstRecoveredTwenty$, more than
seven times the floor. On a stationary control attack
windowing changes accuracy by at most $\numBurstRecoveredFive$ --- below floor, so
\emph{undetectable} rather than zero cost. \textbf{[B1]}

\textbf{Sleepers. Refuted, and in the opposite direction.} We predicted a sleeper
would defeat reputation decay while AIP's windowed statistics reclassified it.
Reputation decay degrades \emph{least} of the memory-carrying defenders
($\numSleeperDropReputation$) and AIP-gated \emph{most} ($\numSleeperDropAip$,
windowed $\numSleeperDropAipWindowed$). The spread, $\numSleeperDropSpread$,
barely clears the $\numFloorWorst$ floor --- marginal, and reported as marginal
--- but its direction is unambiguous. A defence estimating a channel's sign per
question is more disturbed by a regime change than one averaging a scalar over
rounds. \textbf{[E1]}

\textbf{Sybil blocs.} At the largest bloc tested, $s=\numSybilSizeMax$ --- a
plurality of the swarm --- windowed AIP holds $\numSybilCoherentAip$ against a
coherent bloc where the best non-AIP defender reads $\numSybilCoherentBaselines$,
a separation of more than four times the floor (Figure~\ref{fig:sybil}).
\textbf{[E2a]} \emph{But the mechanism we proposed is refuted}: we predicted the
robustness comes from bloc coherence being amplified into a stronger inversion
signal, which predicts a gap between a coherent bloc and one whose members
disagree. There is none --- $\numSybilCoherentAip$ against
$\numSybilDisagreeingAip$, far below floor. The robustness is real; the
explanation is not. What the data is consistent with is simpler:
receiver-anchored statistics do not care how many identities a bloc presents,
because they are not counting. \textbf{[E2b]}

\subsection{What the defence costs}

A defence that has to be argued for on robustness grounds should also be priced.
Two costs, kept separate because they differ by three orders of magnitude.

\emph{Aggregation} cost is measured by timing each rule over the cached
broadcasts, in microseconds per receiver-decision --- one aggregation per
question per agent, which is what a deployed agent pays. It is not recoverable
from our phase logs, which record one timestamp per benchmark-composition block
covering all fourteen rules at once, so it was measured directly rather than
apportioned from a block average. \emph{Inference} cost is read from the cache
and is identical for every rule: all of them consume the same broadcasts.

The ordering is the useful part. AIP-gated is more expensive than majority vote
by a factor of about twenty-five and cheaper than the geometric median by a
factor of eight, and the \emph{gate itself} --- the binomial test, the ceiling
comparison and the signed reweighting --- accounts for

on maintaining channel statistics, which the inversion-free ablation also pays.
Whatever the case against threshold-gated inversion is, it is not compute.

\subsection{Methodological controls}

\textbf{Normalization parity.} The unweighted and discard family is exactly
parity-invariant (maximum $\numParityUnweightedMax$ across every cell), the
sanity check that the audit instrument is not manufacturing its own effect; every
weighted method responds, so self-vote share is a real confound. \textbf{[M2a]}
\emph{Refuted}: AIP does exploit it at the tie. Away from $f=0.5$ the effect is
undetectable (largest $\numParityAipOffTieMax$); at $f=0.5$ it averages
$\numParityAipTieMean$ and reaches $\numParityAipTieMax$, so with the swarm split
evenly the normalisation of the adversary's own vote decides the outcome. Every
number we state at the tie, the evasion anchor included, is measured in the
matched-parity condition. The pooled average $\numParityAipGated$ hides this and
should not be quoted alone. \textbf{[M2b]}

\textbf{Minimax regret} over unknown $f$, deployable methods only: AIP-gated is
best on GSM8K ($\numRegretGsmkAip$ against $\numRegretGsmkNext$) and MMLU
($\numRegretMmluAip$ against $\numRegretMmluNext$), and \emph{not} on MATH-500
($\numRegretMathfiveAip$ against $\numRegretMathfiveNext$). \textbf{[M1]}
