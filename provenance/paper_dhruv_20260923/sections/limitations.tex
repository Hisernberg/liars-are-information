\section{Scope and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

This section collects every claim this study set out to make and did not, every
claim it makes only under conditions, and the instrumentation failures that
produced two retracted verdicts. It is placed before reproducibility rather than
after the conclusion because the refutations are results.

\subsection{The measurement floor, and what it forbids}

The floor is $\numFloorGsmk$ on GSM8K and $\numFloorWorst$ elsewhere
(\S\ref{sec:introduction}). Applying it consistently cost this paper three
findings --- an $N$-scaling result, a mitigation verdict and a Sybil mechanism ---
which is the point of fixing the rule before seeing which way it cuts.

\subsection{Refuted: claims we set out to make and cannot}

Two refutations are given in prose because they revise the paper's own headline
and because a table cannot carry the qualification each needs. The remaining
eight are tabulated in Table~\ref{tab:refutations}; tabulating them raises rather
than lowers their visibility, and every row carries its magnitude and its floor.

\textbf{Accuracy is not flat in the Byzantine fraction.} This is the claim the
study was built to make, and it does not survive. Under the real-LLM coherent
adversary AIP-gated falls by $\numHoneFallBoolq$ on BoolQ, $\numHoneFallMedqa$ on
MedQA, $\numHoneFallMmlu$ on MMLU and $\numHoneFallArc$ on ARC between $f=0$ and
$f=0.7$, all at least three times the floor. GSM8K ($\numHoneFallGsmk$) and
MATH-500 ($\numHoneFallMathfive$) are undetectable, so flatness is there neither
established nor refuted --- which is a weaker statement than either ``flat'' or
``not flat'', and is the one the data supports. Under the symbolic adversary
MATH-500 spans $\numMathfiveAipSpread$. What survives is invariance on specific
benchmarks under a specific adversary, and we state both names wherever it
appears. \textbf{[H1]}

\textbf{An earlier evasion anchor is withdrawn, and a deterrence result with it.}
The attacker gain this project first reported was read off the $f=0.7$ row, where
the Byzantine bloc is an outright majority and every coherence level including
independent lying drives accuracy to zero; that row cannot separate an evasion
effect from numerical dominance and is not an admissible anchor. The anchored
value is $\numEvasionGainMedqa$ at the tie. Separately, an adaptive-adversary
deterrence experiment is absent from this paper rather than reported with a
caveat: the condition for including it, fixed before the audit ran, was that the
coordinatability claim survive re-verification, and it did not. Its data is in
the artifact. \textbf{[G1, B2]}

\subsection{Undetectable at this scale}

\textbf{Whether inversion gain depends on swarm size.} At $N=\numAgents$, $20$
and $50$ the gain reads $\numInversionGainNTen$, $\numInversionGainNTwenty$ and
$\numInversionGainNFifty$: a range of $\numInversionGainNRange$ against a
$\numFloorWorst$ floor. Neither growth nor invariance in $N$ is supported, and
the figure caption says so rather than asserting the flat reading its point
estimates suggest. \textbf{[N1]}

\subsection{Claims we explicitly do not make}

\textbf{Accuracy is not monotone increasing in $f$}, as an early simulation
predicted: only the ungated variant shows it, and that variant is catastrophic at
low $f$. \textbf{[U1]}

\textbf{No generality beyond this population.} $\numModels$ models
($\numParamsMinB$--$\numParamsMaxB$B) plus a $\numModelsWeakArm$-model weak arm,
$\numBenchmarks$ benchmarks, $\numTasksMin$--$\numTasksMax$ tasks each, one
adversary family per attack class, one hardware regime. The honest-coincidence
ceilings must be re-measured for any other population. \textbf{[U2]}

\textbf{No claim that the escape classes survive.} Neither the
external-information nor the longitudinal defence of Section~\ref{sec:limits} has
been evaluated against the gate-aware adversary. \textbf{[U3]} And blind
bootstrapping is not established everywhere: the multiclass law does not beat the
binary law on MMLU ($\numMaeMmluBinary$ against $\numMaeMmluMc$).

\subsection{Where the mechanism fails by design}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Incoherent adversaries.} No coherent channel, nothing to invert; a
      discard method is the correct choice. \textbf{[I2]}
\item \textbf{Binary answer spaces.} Coherence is unity by construction and
      carries no information; majority vote beats AIP on BoolQ by
      $\numRealSepBoolqFseven$.
\item \textbf{Non-stationary adversaries.} Pooled statistics are defeated;
      windowing mitigates but does not eliminate. \textbf{[B1]}
\item \textbf{Partial observability.} Accuracy falls from
      $\numPobsFullGsmkFsix$ at $p_{obs}=1$ to $\numPobsHalfGsmkFsix$ at
      $p_{obs}=0.5$ on GSM8K at $f=0.6$. This, not the Byzantine fraction, is the
      binding practical constraint.
\item \textbf{Gate-aware adversaries.} Section~\ref{sec:gateaware}, and the
      reason is structural rather than incidental (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Declared data gaps}

The binary answer-space class carries no calibrated honest receiver-conditioned
coincidence, so BoolQ's ceiling is a placeholder rather than a measurement and no
margin-over-honest claim is made for it. This is recorded as a gap rather than
absorbed into the results. \textbf{[X1]}

\subsection{Instrumentation failures and retracted verdicts}

Two mitigation variants were initially inert and returned results identical to
the unmitigated baseline. Those identical results were read as a finding---that
the mitigations do not matter---and published internally as such. They were bugs:
M1 fell through to the hard gate for every adversarial channel, and M2's jitter
reached only a fixed-ceiling ablation rather than the default rule. Both verdicts
were retracted.

They were caught by probe tests that assert variant activity before any verdict
is read. We report this beyond mere disclosure: a mitigation study in which a
true null and a wiring bug produce the same observation cannot tell them apart
from the output alone, and an artifact that detects its own instrumentation
failures is stronger evidence than one that cannot. A related error accounts for
three refutations above --- comparisons across mismatched bases (six benchmarks
against three, an upper-bound estimator against a measured one, an $f=0.7$ row
against an $f=0.5$ claim) where the mismatch and not the effect produced the
result. The audit that found them runs in the build. \textbf{[R1]}
