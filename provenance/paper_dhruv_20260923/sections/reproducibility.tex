\section{Reproducibility and Conclusions}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

\subsection{Artifact}

All generation happens once and is written to parquet; aggregation, baselines,
mitigations and analysis read that cache and never invoke a model. That is what
makes a sweep of this size affordable on one device, and what makes the offline
attacks exact --- \texttt{falsified\_confidence} and \texttt{burst} are transforms
of a cached adversarial run, byte-identical to their control outside the one
channel each manipulates. Batch-invariant attention is enabled for every
cache-generating run; without it identical batches return logprobs differing at
the sixth decimal, enough to flip a near-tie argmax. Seeds derive from a SHA-256
of the canonical configuration, task identifiers are frozen and committed, and
every run writes a manifest with its configuration hash, git commit, seeds, GPU
and package versions. All results come from a single hardware regime in bfloat16;
an earlier regime with a different model population is not mixed in, for any
claim.

A completion budget is a measurement instrument, and every benchmark here commits
its answer at the \emph{end} of the completion, so a truncated generation is the
one missing its answer. Auditing by \texttt{finish\_reason} rather than by parse
rate --- 100\% throughout, and not a health metric --- found
\numTruncPinnedBefore{} of \numCacheRows{} generations at the cap and
\numManufacturedBefore{} whose answer the extractor had read out of unfinished
working (\numManufacturedMathfive{} on MATH-500). We raised every budget to
\numBudgetTokens{} tokens and regenerated those rows alone at the same prompts
and seeds, checking per cell that untouched rows came back byte-identical.
Ledger entry X3 itemises what moved; every number here is post-repair.

Every number here is a macro emitted from the result parquets --- a hand-typed
decimal fails the build --- and the ledger, the section-to-claim map and the
manuscript are checked against each other mechanically: a cited claim must exist
in the ledger, a ledger entry must map to a section, no claim may remain
unaudited against the floor, and every figure must name the script that produced
it. Those checks caught four constants from an earlier regime surviving inside
macros, and seven stale figures from it. The release contains the registry, the
frozen task lists, the cache, every result parquet, the generators, the ledger,
the pending-claim audit, the technical note supporting Proposition~1, and the
probe tests of Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. \scaffold{Archive DOI pending
de-anonymisation.}

\subsection{Conclusions}

The 50\% ceiling is a property of discarding, not of the problem. Correlated error
caps what honest pooling can buy --- established for evaluation
panels~\cite{judgecorr,panelcorr}, confirmed here in an adversarial swarm and
extended to the replicated-model case --- which leaves adversarial coherence as
the only informative structure a swarm has that its honest members do not already
share. Inversion is worth $\numInversionGainFseven$ at $f=0.7$ and costs
$\numInversionCostFzero$, below the floor, at $f=0$. Coherence alone cannot
license it, because honest agents cohere at $\numHonestQMc$ against chance
$\numMmluChanceQ$ on the powered four-way multiple-choice pairs; the gate is
calibrated per answer space and anchored on the
receiver's own answer, which lets it work past the point where consensus-based
estimators have converged on the lie.

The limit is not reachable by threshold engineering. An adversary that reads the
published ceiling and coordinates below it drives AIP to $\numEvasionAccMedqa$ at
the tie, with an optimum at $\numEvasionQMedqa$ --- below the chance coherence
$\numEvasionChance$ of honest independent failure. Escaping that needs
information the channel does not contain: external verification, which the
systems literature names as open, or longitudinal statistics, which our sleeper
result gestures toward and which we have not tested. Both are hypotheses, and we
state them as such.
