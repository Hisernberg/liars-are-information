\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We organise prior work by what it does with a suspicious contribution:
\emph{discard} it, \emph{model} it, or \emph{punish} it. Our mechanism belongs to
none of these, because it \emph{uses} the contribution. Two further subsections
cover the measurement result we build on and the theoretical result we
complement.

\subsection{The discard paradigm}

Robust aggregation for distributed learning set the template: Krum selects the
vector minimising summed distance to its neighbours~\cite{blanchard2017krum},
coordinate-wise median and trimmed mean achieve order-optimal rates under a
bounded adversarial fraction~\cite{yin2018trimmed}, geometric median generalises
the same intuition. All are designed for $f<1/2$ and all assume the adversarial
contribution carries no usable information. Within LLM multi-agent systems the
template recurs at the response level, through confidence
weighting~\cite{cpwbft}, receiver-side re-evaluation and filtering~\cite{sac},
certifiable agreement instruments~\cite{hcsc}, learned
aggregation~\cite{laacs}, long-term incentives~\cite{fedmarl} and decentralized
coordination~\cite{decentllms}, within a broader effort to make multi-LLM
networks trustworthy~\cite{trustnetwork} whose threat surface is itself an active
subject~\cite{agentattacks}.

Our results reproduce the expiry: against a symbolic coherent adversary at
$f=0.7$ every \emph{deployable} discard baseline reads
$\numDiscardCollapseFseven$ on all $\numBenchmarks$ benchmarks. Two
qualifications belong here rather than in a footnote, because both weaken the
comparison in our favour if omitted. The collapse to exactly zero is a property
of the symbolic adversary --- against real prompt-injected adversaries nothing
reaches zero, deployable discard spans $\numRealDiscardMinFseven$ to
$\numRealDiscardMaxFseven$, and on BoolQ plain majority vote \emph{beats} AIP by
$\numRealSepBoolqFseven$. And methods handed the true Byzantine count do not
collapse even symbolically: Krum holds $\numDiscardOracleBoolqFseven$ on BoolQ.
We exclude those as undeployable rather than as beaten, and say so wherever the
comparison is made.

\subsection{AgentChain and the identity assumption}

AgentChain~\cite{agentchain} is the closest system-level comparison and is
unusually explicit about its boundary: its guarantees do not extend to
$f \geq N/2$, and it names external identity verification as the unsolved
problem. We cross that line without solving identity, by anchoring the gate on
the receiver's own answer rather than the plurality so the tests do not degrade
when the plurality is adversarial --- a different resource buying a different
guarantee, one Section~\ref{sec:gateaware} shows an adaptive adversary dissolves.
When it dissolves, their gap is exactly where the argument lands. We do not close
it; we give a reason to think a defence in our family \emph{cannot}, which turns
an engineering admission into a statement about the statistic.

\subsection{The correlation ceiling in model panels}

The result our measurement section builds on is not a defence. Kim et
al.~\cite{judgecorr} establish that LLM errors are substantially correlated
across models, and Kohli~\cite{panelcorr} shows what that costs a panel: nine
judges are worth roughly two effective votes, so panel size overstates
reliability. Jiang et al.~\cite{hivemind} document the same homogeneity from the
generation side.

\textbf{We confirm this ceiling and extend it; we do not claim to discover it.}
\emph{First}, the deployment-relevant comparison. Their panels are built from
distinct models; a deployed swarm is more likely built by replicating one, and
that case is roughly half again as correlated ($\numWithinModelPhi$ within-model
against $\numCrossModelPhi$ cross-family). \emph{Second}, from observation to
calibrated law: the multiclass attenuation law is exact at $C=2$ and validated
against that exactness, which turns a descriptive finding into a constant an
aggregator calibrates on at run time without labels. \emph{Third}, correlation as
premise rather than caveat: for a panel the ceiling is a measurement caveat, but
for a swarm under attack it is the premise of the design --- if honest pooling is
capped, the only informative structure left is what honest agents do \emph{not}
share, which is adversarial coherence. Their negative result about panels is what
makes our positive result about inversion necessary rather than merely available.

\subsection{Detection theory: what a richer monitor buys}

The closest theoretical neighbour points the opposite way, and the contrast is
the most useful thing we can say about either. The Detection
Dilemma~\cite{detectiondilemma} establishes in its Theorem~1 that a monitor
observing \emph{four} channels can force honesty: with enough independent views,
misreporting becomes detectable and the rational strategy collapses to truthful
reporting. Our Proposition~1 establishes that a rule measurable with respect to a
\emph{single} per-question coherence statistic is powerless against an adversary
at or below chance coherence, because there the two hypotheses induce the same
distribution.

These bracket the design space from opposite ends --- an existence statement
about monitors against an impossibility statement about one channel --- and are
complementary rather than competing: the question is not whether coherence-based
gating can be repaired but how many independent observations a defence needs, and
for the one-statistic case the answer is more than one. That is also why external
information is the first escape class in Section~\ref{sec:limits}. We do not
evaluate a four-channel monitor and make no claim about whether their bound
survives our gate-aware adversary.

\subsection{Modelling and punishment}

Dawid--Skene~\cite{dawidskene1979} and its descendants treat unreliable
contributors as parameters rather than points to reject. That is closer to our
spirit, but it assumes conditional independence given the truth --- exactly what
a coordinated adversary violates --- and estimates reliability rather than
exploiting the systematic \emph{direction} of error. We use a one-coin variant
inside our blind estimator and as a baseline.

A third line disincentivises adversaries through staking, slashing or reputation
decay, or analyses the strategic setting directly: a Byzantine agent's broadcast
is cheap talk, costless and not inherently credible~\cite{cheaptalk}. This is
orthogonal to aggregation and we make no claim in that direction. We must also
distinguish work on premature convergence in multi-agent
deliberation~\cite{consensustrap}, which operates at \emph{token-level
generation} where shared context collapses diversity before an answer exists. We
operate at \emph{response-level aggregation} on a pathology that is adversarial
rather than conformist; the two are complementary and their mitigations do not
compete.

All inference here is executed offline and cached with vLLM~\cite{vllm}; the
aggregation experiments never invoke a model (Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}).
