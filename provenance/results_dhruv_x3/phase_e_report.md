# Phase E — final analysis, mechanism experiment, and figure set

All offline. No model was loaded. Every number below comes from the deterministic
re-run of both sweeps under gate v2; the seeds are reproducible for the first
time (see §1.1).

## 1. Consistency pass

### 1.1 A reproducibility bug the pass was designed to catch

The delta report compares v1 and v2 of the gate, and its first finding was that
**7,360 cells belonging to methods the gate cannot touch had changed**. That is
impossible from a mechanism change, so the swarms themselves must have differed.
They did: sweep seeds were derived with Python's builtin `hash()` on a config
tuple, and Python randomises string hashing per process unless `PYTHONHASHSEED`
is pinned. Every run of the "same" configuration drew a different swarm while
logging the same seed.

This is fixed — `aip.harness.manifest.stable_seed` derives seeds from a SHA-256
of the canonical config — and **every sweep was re-run from scratch** so all paper
numbers come from one mechanism version *and* one seeding regime. The 7,360
changed cells are retained in the delta report as a measured **seed-noise floor**.

> **Correction.** An earlier revision of this report stated that floor as 0.030.
> That figure was hand-typed and is wrong. Recomputed from
> `results/aggregation/gate_version_delta.parquet`, the distribution of |Δ| over
> the 14,256 non-AIP cells is: median **0.0014**, 90th pct 0.100, **95th pct
> 0.225**, max 0.950. The distribution is extremely heavy-tailed — most cells
> barely move, while high-`f` cells swing enormously because a single agent
> flipping between honest and Byzantine changes the outcome outright. The
> conclusions are unaffected and in fact strengthened: the GSM8K (0.013) and MMLU
> (0.020) gate deltas sit far below even the median-plus-noise scale, while
> MATH-500 (0.493) clears the 95th percentile. Every number in the manuscript is
> now generated by `scripts/make_numbers.py` rather than typed, which is what
> caught this.

**GSM8K and MMLU headlines survive the gate change unchanged**: both stay flat in
`f` and both deltas sit inside the seed-noise floor, so they are not attributable
to the mechanism at all.

**MATH-500 is the known case and it is a genuine trade.** v2 buys +0.194 at f=0
and +0.289 at f=0.1 — the low-`f` bleed that v1 suffered from inverting honest
channels 82–100% of the time — and pays −0.21 to −0.49 above f=0.4. Honest
channels wrongly inverted went from 82–100% to **0% at every f on all three
benchmarks**. I would take that trade for a defence, since inverting honest
agents on the questions they find hardest is the worse failure, but the claim
"flat in f on MATH-500" is withdrawn and listed as UNSUPPORTED in the ledger.

## 2. Mechanism experiment: windowed channel statistics vs burst

Two versions, static and windowed, `W` in {5, 10, 20}, then stopped as scoped.

**Success criterion met, by a wide margin.** Recovering half the burst loss
required +0.015; the windowed gate recovered **+0.225 to +0.259**:

| variant | burst accuracy | recovered | criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| AIP static | 0.676 | — | — |
| AIP windowed W=5 | 0.921 | +0.245 | **meets** (needed +0.015) |
| AIP windowed W=10 | 0.932 | +0.256 | **meets** |
| AIP windowed W=20 | **0.935** | +0.259 | **meets** |

Stationary controls are undamaged: `semantic_negation` moves −0.010 to −0.028
against a CI width of 0.098, and `noise` *improves* by +0.166 to +0.181.

The mechanism is not the regime flag, which never fired (0 flags). It is the
window itself. Under burst the pooled gate trusted **every** adversary channel;
the windowed gate discards all of them, because in a noise epoch the channel is
incoherent and in a coherent epoch it is caught. Demotion to discard rather than
inversion is what makes this safe: a channel caught changing what it is should not
be trusted with a sign.

That the window also improves the pure-noise case by ~0.17 suggests the benefit
is broader than burst — pooled history dilutes any adversary whose behaviour
varies. I am flagging that rather than pursuing it; it is outside this
experiment's scope.

## 3. MMLU decomposition — and a correction to Phase D

**I got this wrong in Phase D and the decomposition corrects it.** I wrote that
AIP's MMLU loss followed because "the gate correctly never fires". It fires:

| benchmark | honest channels inverted | adversary channels inverted (f≥0.5) |
|---|---|---|
| gsm8k | **0%** | 100% |
| math500 | **0%** | 83% |
| mmlu | **0%** | 100% |

The gate's targeting on MMLU is as good as anywhere. The confusion was between
two coherence measures: the *cross-model* `q̂` (do llama and ministral tell the
same lie?) and the *within-swarm* coherence the gate computes, which is near 1.0
between Byzantine agents replaying the same model's cache.

The real cause is **coordinatability**:

| benchmark | class | negation `q̂` | honest floor | above honest? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gsm8k | open_numeric | **0.620** | 0.597 | yes |
| math500 | open_text | **0.230** | 0.143 | yes |
| mmlu | multiple_choice | **0.290** | **0.535** | **no** |

On MMLU the adversary is *less* coherent than honest models naturally are. Two
adversaries told to report "the most contrary option" disagree about which
option that is, where "report 2×V" is computable identically by both. So the
channels are inverted correctly but the inversion carries little information:
pushing evidence away from several *different* wrong answers spreads the positive
mass over the remaining options, including other wrong ones, instead of
concentrating it on the truth.

**A structured lie needs a rule the liars can compute identically, and closed
label spaces make that harder, not easier.** That is the coordinatability result,
and it is the opposite of the intuition that a small answer space makes collusion
easy.

## 4. Limitations

Each failure with its measured cost and the condition under which it applies.

| failure | quantitative cost | scope condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Burst (non-stationary)** | −0.207 with the pooled gate; recovered to +0.259 by a window of 20 | Applies whenever the adversary's regime changes faster than the statistics pool. Mitigated, not eliminated: the window is a fixed hyper-parameter, and an adversary that switches on the window's own timescale is untested. |
| **Noise (incoherent)** | AIP −0.253 vs best discard on GSM8K with the static gate | Applies whenever no coherent channel exists. AIP has nothing to invert and its machinery adds variance; a discard method is the correct choice. The windowed variant closes most of this gap (+0.17). |
| **MATH-500 high f** | −0.21 to −0.49 versus gate v1 above f=0.4 | Applies to open-text answer spaces where the adversary draws its lie from the honest wrong-answer distribution, so agreement clusters overlap and the cluster split cannot separate them. |
| **Partial observability** | 0.983 → 0.865 at p_obs=0.5 (GSM8K, f=0.6) | Applies whenever a receiver hears a peer on too few tasks to establish bloc membership. This is the binding practical constraint on AIP, more than `f` is. |
| **MMLU coordinatability** | −0.03 to −0.32 across attacks | Applies to closed label spaces where honest coincidence is high (1/(C−1) chance floor) and adversaries cannot agree on a lie. Inversion is correct but uninformative. |
| **Honest-q calibration** | ceilings moved 0.775 → 0.416 (open-numeric) and 0.042 → 0.198 (open-text) as measurement improved | The ceilings are a property of *this* model population and task type. They must be re-measured for any new population; they are not universal constants. |
